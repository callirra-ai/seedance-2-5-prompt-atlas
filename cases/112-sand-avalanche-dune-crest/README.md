## Prompt

```text
Grains of fine sand slip from the crest of a small dune and start a slow avalanche, the slope collapsing in sheets that chase each other downhill. The camera pulls back and up in one continuous crane move, widening until the whole dune fits the frame. Harsh low sun skims the ridges, throwing long shadows and lighting the airborne dust into a golden haze. Late afternoon desert, warm ochre tones, every ripple crisp. Audio: a soft dry hiss as the grains run, thinning as the slope settles.
```

## Why it works

Ten seconds and a wide frame are needed because an avalanche is a process, not a beat: the sheets have to start, chase each other and settle. One continuous crane-out does the storytelling, shifting the shot from texture to scale without a cut. Low raking sun is doing the heavy lifting — it makes the airborne dust visible as haze and gives each advancing sheet a lit leading edge — and the audio is written to thin out as the slope settles so the sound matches the picture's ending.
