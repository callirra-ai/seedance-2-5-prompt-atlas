## Prompt

```text
Match the reference image exactly: same stall, same griddle, same vendor. Skewers turn over glowing charcoal, fat drips and flares, and a hand shakes chilli over the row while steam rolls upward. The camera begins tight on the coals and tilts slowly up the counter to the vendor as they call out to someone off screen. Orange charcoal light glows from below and a cold work lamp hangs above, greasy highlights on steel. Warm 35mm. Audio: sizzle, fat pops, a fan, a shout.
```

## Why it works

The tilt is a simple contract: start on coals, end on the face, and the model can interpolate everything between those two anchors. Charcoal underglow is warm from below while a cold lamp hangs above, so the frame has two light directions instead of the flat frontal flash look most stall photos arrive with. The audio cue carries the sense of heat a still image cannot.

## Reference image

Upload a photo of a night food stall taken close to the griddle, with flame or coals visible and the vendor hands-on. Avoid flash-lit frames with flat frontal light, because the shot depends on the warm underglow the coals already give you.
