## Prompt

```text
A single drop of clear face serum leaves the glass dropper and falls onto the back of a hand, the bottle behind it untouched and its label fully readable. The camera descends from directly overhead, stopping just short of contact, so skin texture and the wet edge stay sharp. Light comes from a large frosted panel at upper left, wrapping the scene in shadowless midday softness that keeps the branding crisp. Cool neutral tones, glass and liquid rendered cleanly, no glare on the packaging.
```

## Why it works

Beauty shots fail in two ways: invented skin texture and a label that slides, so the prompt fixes both by describing the light as shadowless and frontal and by naming the label as readable rather than decorative. The overhead descent stops just short of contact deliberately — a completed impact turns into unreadable splatter inside five seconds, while a drop that is still falling keeps its shape and its highlight.

## Reference image

A close photo of the serum bottle with the dropper in frame and the label square to the lens. Avoid photos with hard reflections across the label, fingerprints on the glass or a busy background, since those artefacts get animated along with everything else.
