## Prompt

```text
A steel pick taps a clear block of ice once and a hairline crack races across the interior, splitting the block into two clean halves. The camera holds on the crack line and punches in fast at the exact moment of fracture, then settles. A hard cold beam from directly above drills through the ice so the fracture flashes white while the outer faces stay frosted. Late winter, the air visibly cold, no background detail. Audio: a low creak, a sharp crack, then ice chips tinkling onto metal.
```

## Why it works

Fracture is a single-frame event, so the prompt anchors the camera to it: hold, then punch in exactly at the split. Without that instruction the model tends to spread the break over the whole clip and the impact disappears. Top-down hard light is specified because transmitted light is what makes the crack flash white against the frosted faces, and the three-part audio cue — creak, crack, tinkle — gives the moment a clear pre-roll and tail that an editor can sync to.
