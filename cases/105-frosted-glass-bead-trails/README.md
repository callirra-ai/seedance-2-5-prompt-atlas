## Prompt

```text
Tiny beads of condensation swell on the outside of a frosted tumbler, one growing heavy enough to break and drag a clean line down the glass. The camera eases in from a wide view until the frame holds nothing but beads and the wet trails they leave. Cold dawn light arrives at a very low grazing angle, so each bead is rimmed and throws a tiny soft shadow across the etched surface. Vertical framing, shallow focus, dust-free glass, ice visible as blurred white shapes behind. Quiet and clinical.
```

## Why it works

This is the deliberately cheap version: 480p suits a subject made of soft bokeh, and a vertical frame matches how satisfying-physics clips are actually watched. The physics are named as a sequence — swell, break, drag a line — because a bead that simply exists is static, while a bead that breaks gives the shot its event. Extreme grazing light is the trick that makes this work at all: it rims every droplet and casts the micro-shadows that read as texture rather than as blur.
