## Prompt

```text
The chilled can of orange sparkling soda from the reference image sits on wet slate, label unaltered, while a hand lifts the ring pull and fine mist escapes. The camera arcs a short quarter-turn right, keeping the label readable. Hard backlight burns through the glass so each rising bubble carries its own highlight against a near-black backdrop with cold metal fill. Condensation beads and slides in slow rivulets, glossy advertising finish. Audio: one crisp crack, then a long fine hiss.
```

## Why it works

Two lines carry this shot: the label is pinned as unchangeable and the camera is limited to one short arc, because long moves are where generated packaging starts to smudge and wobble. Specifying backlight with cold metal fill is what makes the carbonation read — a front-lit can turns into a flat milky fizz. The audio cue is a single crack plus one hiss, a beat a five-second cut can actually land.

## Reference image

A straight-on photo of the can, label centred and fully visible, on an uncluttered surface. Avoid shots where the label is angled away, cropped or blocked by fingers — the model keeps what it is shown, so anything hidden in the photo stays hidden in the video.
