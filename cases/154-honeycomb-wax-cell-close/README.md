## Prompt

```text
The honeycomb stays exactly as in the reference image, its wax walls unbroken, while a thin film of honey creeps slowly down one cell wall and pools at the base. The camera holds a locked macro frame as the focus plane crawls across the cells, bringing each hexagon in and out of sharpness in turn. Light comes from underneath the comb, so the wax glows warm amber from within while the front faces stay in soft shadow. Matte wax, glossy honey, shallow depth, nothing else added.
```

## Why it works

Combs are geometry, and geometry is the first thing generators bend, so the prompt pins the wax walls as unbroken and confines the motion to one small film of honey. The camera move is a focus crawl rather than a dolly, which is how real macro shots of a flat textured surface are actually made and which keeps the hexagons from distorting. Underlighting is the reason this is not just a texture plate: transmitted light turns the wax translucent and gives the honey pooling at each base something to glow against.

## Reference image

A sharp macro photo of honeycomb filling the frame, evenly lit and free of blur at the edges. Avoid photos with bees, hands or jars in shot, and avoid heavy motion blur — the model keeps the comb geometry it is given and animates only the small movement you describe.
