## Prompt

```text
A long silk scarf slips from open hands and falls in slow motion, unfolding and twisting once as it drops. The camera tracks down with the fabric, staying just above it, ending as the hem settles on a bare concrete floor. Golden-hour sun comes through slatted blinds, laying warm horizontal stripes across the silk and the floor while deep shade fills between them. Shot on a long lens, fabric sheen shifting from bright to velvet as it turns. Silent, editorial, calm.
```

## Why it works

Fabric is the hardest common material to generate, so the prompt gives it exactly one deformation to perform — unfold once, twist once — instead of a general instruction to billow. Tracking down with the falling scarf keeps it the same size in frame, which hides the moment where generated cloth usually loses its weave. Striped light through blinds is doing two jobs: it sells the golden hour and it gives the silk hard edges to catch, revealing sheen changes that flat light would hide.
