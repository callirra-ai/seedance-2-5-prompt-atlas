## Prompt

```text
A lean chestnut horse runs flat out from left to right across a wide summer meadow, mane streaming, tail lifted, hooves cutting through long grass and throwing up seeds. The camera tracks alongside at the same speed on a long lens, keeping the horse in profile with the horizon steady behind it. The sun sits directly behind the animal, rimming its outline and stretching a long shadow ahead of it. Slight motion blur on the legs, warm dry haze, nothing else in frame, no riders, no fences.
```

## Why it works

Eight seconds is what a full crossing needs, and tracking alongside keeps the horse in profile the entire way, which is the angle where a gallop reads cleanest. The backlight is doing two jobs: it rims the mane and tail, and the long shadow thrown ahead of the horse gives the frame a second moving element that signals speed without blurring the body.
