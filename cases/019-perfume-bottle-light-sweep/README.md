## Prompt

```text
A faceted glass perfume bottle stands on wet black stone, its blank label facing forward. The camera slides left to right at label height, unhurried, revealing cut edges that throw caustic patterns across the stone. One hard beam strikes from behind at a low dusk-blue ambient level, so the liquid inside glows amber while the glass stays dark and reflective. Fine water film and a few droplets catch the beam. Luxury minimalism, deep blacks, no visible text. Silent, product-only.
```

## Why it works

This is the cheap first try: 480p and five seconds, because a light sweep across glass is one of the few product moves that survives a low resolution cleanly. The label is described as blank on purpose — an invented logo is the fastest way to make a luxury spot look fake. Backlighting the liquid while keeping the glass itself dark is what produces the amber core and the caustics, which is a lighting decision, not a grading one, and the camera is restricted to a flat lateral slide so the bottle never rotates away from its silhouette.
