## Prompt

```text
A warehouse ceiling becomes the floor. A dropped wrench stops mid-fall, hangs for a beat, then accelerates upward past a workbench; dust and water droplets reverse direction in the same instant, and a mug of coffee shears sideways into a floating ribbon. The camera rolls a full half-turn with the room and ends upside down, still tracking the wrench. Flickering fluorescent tubes overhead, hard top-light from what is now the floor below.
```

## Why it works

Gravity inversion only reads if ordinary objects reverse before the room does, so the shot leads with a wrench, dust and coffee rather than with the architecture, and the camera rolls half a turn with the environment so the change feels like the world moving rather than the lens. Flickering fluorescents are picked over a clean light because the flicker covers the moment where the model has to reorient everything. This case is written silent on purpose: losing the room tone is what makes the flip feel wrong.
