## Prompt

```text
Keep both passengers, the clothing and the car interior exactly as in the reference image. Parked at night with the engine off; the driver grips the wheel with both hands and stares ahead, says something, then looks down, while the passenger slowly turns to face them. The camera stays outside the windscreen at bonnet height, static, rain beading on the glass. Dashboard glow, one street lamp behind. Rain on the roof and a seatbelt creaking.
```

## Why it works

Shooting through the glass from outside solves the hardest problem in a car scene: you get two people in one frame without fighting the interior for camera space. The performance is written as a sequence of gaze — stare ahead, look down, turn — because where each person is looking carries the confession better than any facial detail a model can generate. Rain beading on the windscreen adds motion the static frame would otherwise lack.

## Reference image

Upload a two-character still shot through a car windscreen or side window, with both faces lit by the dashboard or a street lamp. Avoid daytime images with strong sun and any frame where a number plate or logo is legible — the model reproduces readable text and it will look wrong.
