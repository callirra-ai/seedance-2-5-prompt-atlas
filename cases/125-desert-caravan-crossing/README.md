## Prompt

```text
A camel caravan of twelve crosses a high dune ridge at late afternoon, tiny against the sand, following the line of the crest. The camera starts close on the lead camel's rope halter and pulls back and up into a wide reveal of the erg, one uninterrupted retreat. Hard low sun, long shadows striping the slip faces, fine dust hanging in the air. Coarse wool blankets, brass harness fittings, dry leather, wind-carved sand.
```

## Why it works

A pull-back is the cheapest way to make a landscape feel enormous, but it only works if the first frame has something small and specific to leave behind — here, a rope halter. The wide 21:9 frame is chosen for the reveal rather than the subject, and the low sun gives the sand the ridge shadows that make scale read. No audio: wind noise would add little and silence suits the distance.
